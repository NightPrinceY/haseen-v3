1 · the label

One training row: three yes/no questions per word

A word is not given one dialect label. It gets three bits: is it natural in Egyptian (the customer-service register Yahya validated), in Gulf, in MSA? A bit can be unknown; unknown bits are masked out of the loss, so the labeler never has to guess. The target is chosen at inference: a word deviates when its bit for the target is 0. One row therefore serves all three targets.

  2 · pipeline
  
How the data was built

  
Real sentences come in on the left. A labeler that never guesses tags every word in its own sentence. Word alignment against MSA translations lets the builder swap single words between varieties while keeping the host grammar. Seven families of rows come out, pass the guards and are split. No API model was called; the only compute was 9 minutes on an L4 for alignment.

  5 · architecture
  
Model: an Arabic encoder with three naturalness heads

  
System 1 is a single forward pass. MARBERTv2 (163M parameters, trained on a billion Arabic tweets, dialect-heavy) is warm-started from ALDi-Token-DI, a MARBERT tagger already trained to tell dialect words from MSA words. Each word is read at its first subword. The heads are small; almost all capacity is in the encoder.

  






  6 · heads, loss, decision
  
What we add on top of the encoder

  

    

      
Variety pointer head (the main head)

      
Each variety is a short option text ("مصري", "خليجي", "فصحى"), encoded once by the same encoder and cached. A word scores against each option by scaled cosine, so new varieties can later be added as new options. Each score goes through a sigmoid: the three answers are independent.

      
z_w = normalize(W_w · h_w)          W_w: 768 → 256
o_v = normalize(W_o · enc(text_v))   W_o: 768 → 256, cached
p(natural in v | w) = σ( e^s · z_w·o_v + b_v )
v ∈ {EGY, GLF, MSA}                  s: learned scale

      
Ablation twin: a plain linear layer 768 → 3. In the pilot both reached the same F1; the pointer head was better calibrated (ECE 0.008).

    

    

      
Small heads and the loss

      
p_dia(w)   = σ(u_dia · h_w)    other dialect → "unknown"
p_other(w) = σ(u_oth · h_w)    names, digits, Latin
p_frm(w)   = σ(u_frm · h_w)    formal-register word (aux)

L = Σ_w Σ_v m_wv · BCE(p_v, y_wv)      m_wv = 0 if unknown
  + λ_dia · BCE_dia + λ_oth · BCE_oth + 0.2 · BCE_frm
pos_weight on the 0-class (deviation) ≈ 3

      
Masked BCE is what makes the unknown bits safe: they contribute nothing, in either direction.

    

    

      
Decision layer (no training, a few lines)

      
deviates(w, t) = p_dia > τ_dia  or  p_t(w) < τ_t
label(w, t)    = "unknown" if dia, else the variety v ≠ t
                 with the highest p_v (order: Egyptian→MSA,Gulf;
                 MSA→Egyptian,Gulf; Gulf→MSA,Egyptian)
confidence     = calibrated 1 − p_t(w)   (temperature on dev)

      
τ is tuned on dev, never on gold. A later "strict colloquial Egyptian" policy can flag frm words without retraining.
