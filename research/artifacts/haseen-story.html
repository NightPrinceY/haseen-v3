HaSeen هـ · سين

I built HaSeen to read an Arabic turn and say, for every word, whether it belongs to the dialect the voice agent was told to speak, and what it is when it does not. It runs offline on a CPU, with no LLM. The name is the idea: Egyptian says هيتم, MSA says سيتم; one letter decides the dialect.

01 · The task

What Tuner asked, and what I decided

Given a text and a target dialect, label every word as conforming or deviating and say what a deviating word is instead. No cloud LLM at inference: the system must run air-gapped. Graded on architecture, productisation, ML judgment, code quality, reproducibility and communication.

I sent five questions within hours of receiving the task; Mohamed answered all five in 16 minutes. The "your call" answers became decisions I state in the README.

My questionAnswerWhat I did
A word valid in both the target and MSA: conforming?YesOne naturalness bit per variety; a word deviates only when the target's bit is 0. Formal service words (الفاتورة، التحويل) conform everywhere.
Does morphology count (هـ، بـ، ما…ش)?Your callPositive evidence only: سوف and سـ+verb deviate in Egyptian, the هـ-future deviates outside Egypt; a word that only lacks a prefix conforms.
CPU or GPU? Self-hosted model?GPU, yesI still made it run on CPU (7 ms int8), because a guard before TTS runs on every turn.
Arabizi / code-switching?Your callLatin script, digits and names always conform and are not scored.
LLMs to build data offline?Your callOnly for evaluation candidates and Claude-assisted labelling that I verified; training labels come from expert lexicons, rules and corpus evidence.

Targets I set before any work

Deviation F1 ≥ 0.85; p95 latency under 20 ms on CPU; one command from a fresh clone to a working CLI. PRD

Register

The target is a dialect as a customer-service agent speaks it: semi-formal. I fixed this with 42 yes/no checks on real cases, as a native Egyptian speaker.

Scope

The task asked for at least one dialect. I shipped six targets, because Tuner's customers are spread across the Gulf, Egypt, the Levant and Iraq.

03 · Research

What the field already knew

Before choosing anything I read the papers, listed every dataset with its licence, and ran three small spikes (about $0.03) to check what really exists. These findings shaped everything after.

LLMs drift out of dialect

In the AMIYA shared task, a baseline LLM answered in Egyptian only 11% of the time when asked to. Sentence-level scores cannot say which word broke the dialect; a word-level detector can.

LLM judges are weak at dialect

GPT-4 scores about 46 macro-F1 on dialect identification; fine-tuned MARBERT about 83. So the decider had to be a trained encoder, not a prompted LLM.

Word lists are not enough

Published Gulf cue lists had precision 0.0 on multi-dialect data; 56% of dialect sentences are valid in several regions (NADI 2024 moved to multi-label). Context and multi-label output are required.

No word-level data to download

LinCE MSA–Egyptian was offline. So I had to build labelled data myself, from parallel corpora and expert lexicons.

Morphology alone fails

"Deviating if the Egyptian analyzer cannot parse it" scored F1 0.16: the Egyptian database analyzes 99% of MSA words. Rules became evidence for labels, not the decider.

A good warm start exists

VARabi/ALDi-Token-DI already tags MSA vs dialect per token (MARBERT). It misses a single سوف inside Egyptian, so my data had to teach single-word slips.

MARBERT tokenizes dialect best

On 30K Egyptian words: 1.86 sub-words per word and 2.3% unknown, against far worse for MSA-only models.

An easy bench lies

Found later, in my own work: a bench made of the same corpus family as training overstated Build 2 (0.932 → 0.819 on fresh speech). Build 3's test was built first, from new sources.

Training recipe

OptimiserAdamW, weight decay 0.01; encoder lr 4e-5, heads lr 1e-3; linear warm-up 6%, then linear decay; gradient clip 1.0
Batch, length32 sentences, up to 128 sub-words; fp16 mixed precision on one NVIDIA L4
Schedule1 epoch over 501,080 sentences = 15,659 steps (the dev curve is flat after about 13K steps)
Lossmasked binary cross-entropy on the 7 naturalness bits (unknown = weight 0, "not natural" weighted ×3) + other-dialect + non-Arabic + 0.2 × register
Calibrationtemperature scaling on dev (LBFGS), then one threshold per target under a 1% false-alarm cap
ExportONNX opset 17, dynamic int8 per-tensor quantisation (per-channel was tested and rejected: 81.6% agreement)

The Build-3 labeler review

A type-level diff of old and new labels on the 20,000 most frequent training words showed what each rule changed; two rules that hurt were rolled back before training.

was read as عند and called MSA. Exact MADAR city forms now win over clitic stripping.

leaks into Yemeni and Saudi text at 2–3% of its Egyptian rate. A leak no longer counts as use.

the هـ-future is Egyptian only; هيدي، هيبه are not futures.

was "not MSA" because Iraqi text uses it far more. Names used in MSA text stay MSA.

09 · Training and choice

Twenty runs, failures kept

I wrote the expected result before every round and kept every run, including the one that collapsed. Fixes came from dev data, never from the test.

  1. 1

    Build 1: a collapse, then 0.963

    The first pointer head collapsed: its three option vectors were nearly identical, so it learned one "naturalness" score. I gave each option a learned residual and it trained. Three seeds: Egyptian F1 0.966 ± 0.005, 74 behaviour tests, ECE 0.0005. Shipped the seed with the best dev score, not the best gold, to keep the gold clean.

  2. 2

    Build 2: nine varieties, then a gate I added

    Four runs in parallel on one L4, then three more after labeler fixes. The run with the best dev F1 failed the task example (أبغى), so I added a known-answer gate and shipped the only run that passed 54/54, recording the deviation from my own rule.

  3. 3

    Build 3, round 1: stopped at 0.83, and I asked why

    The training curve was flat, so more epochs would not help. I scored the labeler itself on dev: 0.815. The model had reached its teacher's ceiling.

    teacher, round 1model, round 1teacher, round 2model, round 2
  4. 4

    Build 3, round 2: review first, then train

    I did not start the GPU until the data was right: labeler review, a clean rebuild (an earlier build was corrupted by two writers and discarded), the leakage filter, a label spot check, and the whole pipeline run end to end on a tiny model. Round 2: dev 0.876, known answers 71/73 (round 1 had failed at 69/73).

  5. 5

    The choice

    Rule written before the results: pass the known-answer gate, then the higher dev F1. Linear 0.876 vs pointer 0.871 → linear. Exported to int8, thresholds refit on the int8 outputs, test scored once.

10 · Results · fresh test, scored once, the int8 model that ships

Precise everywhere, strongest on MSA and Egyptian

All six targets stay under 1% false alarms on native speech, and what HaSeen flags is right 91–97% of the time. MSA and Egyptian pass the F1 ≥ 0.90 gate; Saudi and Gulf are within 0.02 of it; Levantine and Iraqi miss about a quarter of swapped words. Dev 0.874, test 0.869: no sign of over-fitting.

13 · Limits and next

Where it is weak, and what I would do

  • Egyptian words under target Gulf. In a probe of 31 frequent Egyptian service words, Gulf flags 8 (حضرتك، دلوقتي، هيوصل) and misses عايز، إحنا، أوي، مفيش، برضه. Some of the 31 are also Hijazi, but most misses are real. Cause: Gulf is "not natural" only when Saudi, Gulf-east and Yemeni all say no, and Yemeni is often unknown. Fix: treat that unknown as no when Saudi and Gulf-east say no and Yemeni text barely uses the word, then retrain (about 1.5 hours, $1.3).
  • Words it has never seen. Swaps from held-out concepts are caught 32–63% of the time; held-out dictionary entries 57%. Next: more lexicon coverage for Levantine and Iraqi.
  • The first label. The label set holds the right variety 83–92% of the time, its first item 38–79%. Next: rank labels by how specific a variety is to the word.
  • LLM-style replies. Agent-set F1 0.49–0.71; the agent labels are mine with Claude's help, not checked by native speakers of every dialect. Next: native reviewers per dialect, starting with Gulf.
  • Licences. MADAR, SADA, Birzeit and QCRI sets are research or non-commercial, so this release is private and for research. Production: the same pipeline rebuilt on commercial-friendly data and Tuner's own call transcripts.
  • Text only. Accent and pronunciation reach HaSeen through STT output; running it on ASR transcripts is the natural next integration.
