Results · HaSeen-3-Bench test · thresholds fitted on dev

Precise everywhere, strongest on MSA and Egyptian

All six targets stay under 1% false alarms on native speech, and what HaSeen flags is right 91–97% of the time. MSA and Egyptian pass the F1 ≥ 0.90 gate; Saudi and Gulf are within 0.02 of it; Levantine and Iraqi miss about a quarter of swapped words.

natural = native sentences, every word should pass · challenge = 1–3 words swapped for another variety's word for the same MADAR concept, plus MSA-drift injections · label set = the true source variety is among the labels. Files: test results, policy, same test through the package.

V3 against V2, same test

V2 scored 0.932 on its own bench, built from the same kind of sentences it trained on. On this fresher test it scores 0.819, with V2's thresholds re-fitted on the V3 dev exactly as I fitted V3's. V3 is better on every target, and V2 cannot keep MSA under 1% false alarms.

How I got here

The model was never the bottleneck

  1. 1

    A fresh test first

    V2 looked strong (0.932), but its bench came from the same corpus family as its training data. For V3 I built the test before any training data: Saudi TV shows (speaker-disjoint from training), Egyptian speech, Al Jazeera, Levantine and Iraqi corpora, LLM agent replies, and 900 held-out dictionary entries. 15,357 sentences across dev and test, frozen with a sha256 manifest. Bench card

  2. 2

    Round 1 stopped at 0.83, and I asked why

    More epochs would not help: the training curve was flat. So I scored the labeler itself (the teacher that writes the training labels) on the same dev. It scored 0.815. The model had reached its teacher's ceiling.

    teacher v9model, round 1teacher v10model, round 2
  3. 3

    I fixed the gold before the model

    The MSA test held colloquial talk-show sentences labelled as clean MSA («مصر هي اللي صدرت…»), so correct flags counted as errors. I read all 558 MGB-2 sentences of the MSA sets and removed 70, by the same rule on dev and test, without looking at any model output for test. Script

  4. 4

    Then I rebuilt the teacher, word by word

    A type-level diff of old and new labels on the 20,000 most frequent training words showed what each rule changed. Rules that hurt were rolled back (a corpus rate cannot overrule a crowd lexicon, because Iraqi text is news-heavy).

    لعند
    was read as عند and called MSA. Exact MADAR city forms now win over clitic stripping.
    حضرتك
    leaks into Yemeni and Saudi text at 2–3% of its Egyptian rate. A leak no longer counts as use.
    هيوصل
    the هـ-future is Egyptian only; هيدي and هيبه are not futures.
    العراق
    was "not MSA" because Iraqi text uses it far more. Names used in MSA text stay MSA.
    سيتم
    a سـ-future is a hard MSA marker even where Saudi TV uses it.

    Teacher on dev: 0.815 → 0.864, false alarms ≤ 0.81% everywhere. Labeler

  5. 5

    A release gate I could fail

    Besides false alarms and F1, every candidate must pass the task example and 95% of 73 known-answer checks. Round 1 failed it (69/73: a Gulf agent saying حضرتك went unflagged). Round 2 passed with 71/73, and I chose the run by the rule I wrote before the results. Pre-registration

  6. 6

    Ship the model you measured

    int8 ONNX keeps 99.41% of the fp32 decisions; I fitted the thresholds on the int8 outputs themselves, then scored the test once: 0.869, against 0.874 on dev.

Architecture

One pass, every target

Why naturalness bits

The target is a runtime choice, so the model never learns "is this Egyptian?". It learns, for every variety, "would a native speaker of it say this word here?". A word deviates when its target bit says no; the labels are the varieties that say yes.

Why Gulf contains Saudi

Agents are configured as "Khaleeji" or as "Saudi". Gulf accepts a word natural in any Gulf variety; Saudi is stricter. Saudi is never named as the source of a deviation from Gulf.

Why this size

162.3M parameters, 155 MB int8, 7.1 ms on 4 CPU threads. It runs per turn in an eval pipeline, in a Docker container with no network, or in the browser.

Data

Human speech to test, evidence to train

HaSeen-3-Bench

Natural: 6,254 test sentences, 71.3K words. Challenge: 3,484 sentences, 4,996 swapped words; 25% of MADAR concepts never appear in training. Agent: 200 LLM replies asked to speak a dialect. Probe: 900 held-out mo3jam entries.

HaSeen-3-Mix

501,080 training sentences, 6.2M words, one naturalness bit per word and variety: native text (420K), MADAR aligned swaps (13K), lexicon substitutions (56K), MSA-future transforms (12K). No sentence and no 6-gram shared with the test. Data card

New native sources in V3

SADA (Saudi TV, Najdi / Hijazi / Khaliji speakers), MasriSpeech, MGB-3, SDAIA SCC and QCRI ArabicCulturalQA, chosen by a rule: human-transcribed text only in the test, model-transcribed text for training at most, LLM and TTS scripts never.

Limits and what I would do next

Where V3 is weak

  • Egyptian words under target Gulf. In my probe of 31 frequent Egyptian service words, Gulf flags 8 (حضرتك، دلوقتي، هيوصل) and misses عايز، إحنا، أوي، مفيش، برضه. Some of the 31 are also Hijazi, but most misses are real. Cause: Gulf is "not natural" only when Saudi, Gulf-east and Yemeni all say no, and Yemeni is often unknown. Fix for 3.1: treat a Yemeni unknown as no when Saudi and Gulf-east say no and Yemeni text barely uses the word, then retrain.
  • Words it has never seen. Swaps from held-out concepts are caught 32–63% of the time; on held-out dictionary entries, 57%.
  • LLM-style replies. On real agent replies: Gulf F1 0.71, Saudi 0.64, Iraqi 0.51, Levantine 0.49; precision above recall; Iraqi false alarms on 3% of words. These labels are mine (Claude-assisted), not native-verified.
  • The first label. The label set holds the right variety 83–92% of the time; its first item, 38–79%. Ranking by how specific a variety is to the word is the next change.
Product

One command, offline

uv tool install "git+https://github.com/NightPrinceY/haseen-v3"
haseen download                                        # once, 156 MB, then offline
haseen "أنا أبغى أروح الاجتماع بكرة بس سوف أتأخر شوية" -t egy
haseen serve --port 8000                               # POST /detect {"text", "target"}
docker run --rm --network none -p 8000:8000 nightprincey/haseen3:3.0.0

In a voice-agent QA product it becomes a dialect-adherence eval per turn (share of words that fit, plus the words that do not), a red flag when an agent drifts to MSA or another dialect, a caller-dialect estimate from the transcript, and a check that a simulated caller speaks the dialect it was given.
