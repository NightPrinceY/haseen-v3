Ten Arabic targets, chosen by the words and backed by verified data

I studied 14 resources, merged them into one inventory, and measured which varieties differ at the word level. Countries whose words cannot be told apart become one target. Then only verified data counts: ten targets clear that bar and ship; five more are recognised and flagged as "other dialect" until their data does.

For reference

Sentence-level ID is harder than word-level flags

Char 1–5-gram SVM trained and tested inside Alexandria (Libya and Sudan have no training split there). Maghreb is easy; Jordan, Palestine and Saudi blur. Trained on written text and tested on speech transcripts, macro-F1 drops from 0.62 to 0.46, so evaluation must include spoken text.

Why this shape · Tuner

Where HaSeen plugs into Tuner

Tuner sells reliability for voice agents: simulation before launch, evals and alerts after. In the first meeting Mohamed named the gap directly: agents do not stick to the dialect they are expected to use, and today's evals for it are not accurate. Tuner's 21 evals are LLM judges, and its simulator offers "Arabic" as a single language with no dialect, while English gets US, UK and AU.

Dialect adherence eval

Per agent turn: share of words that fit the configured dialect, plus each deviating word and where it belongs. PASS / FAIL by threshold or a 1–5 score, next to Hallucination and Politeness.

Red flags and alerts

"Agent drifted to MSA", "Egyptian agent used Gulf words", tracked per agent version so a prompt change that breaks the dialect shows up the same day.

Caller dialect

The same word bits on STT caller turns give the caller's dialect: a "dialect match" eval (Saudi caller, Egyptian agent) and a new analytics dimension.

Dialect-aware simulation

Lets the simulator offer Arabic (Saudi), (Egyptian), (Levantine) and check that the simulated caller actually speaks it. One encoder, ONNX int8 on CPU, about 11 ms per turn, no LLM call.

Gold · HaSeen-Bench v2

The ship gate for every target

natural · 300 per target

Held-out native sentences from Alexandria test, Casablanca, Habibi, Lisan and Nâbra. Almost nothing should be flagged; a native checker reviews only the flags. Gate: false alarms ≤ 1% of words.

challenge · 300 per target

Target sentences with 1–3 words swapped for MADAR's expert form of the same concept from another variety. Exact positions, no annotator needed. Gate: F1 ≥ 0.90.

agent · 150 per target

Replies written "in dialect T" by several open-weight LLMs, the real failure mode, labelled and then verified by a native speaker. Gate: F1 ≥ 0.85. A target that misses the gate falls back to "other dialect".
